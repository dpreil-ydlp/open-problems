## Key-family relationship

The PDFs describe the same SSC mechanism and use the same standard ordered alphabet and illustrative Figure 1. Their challenge tables are independently described as random assignments. Part 1 and Part 2 have different selection behavior (sequential vs random). No source found says the actual challenge messages share a keyword, offset, table, or full key. Treat them as the same cipher family, not as a shared-key pair.

The Cryptologia abstract says one attack succeeded on the first 201 symbols of a telegram. That length matches Part 1's 201 symbols, so it may refer to the same message; the abstract does not establish identity or give the plaintext. Full article text was not accessible here, so the possible match remains unconfirmed.

## Published plaintext or key

No publicly accessible plaintext/key or solver write-up for either challenge was found in the sources checked. The MysteryTwister listing exposes solve counts, not the submitted answers. This means “not found in the public sources accessed,” not “no solver knows the answer.”
