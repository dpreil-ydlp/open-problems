# A370206: infinitude of diagonal meetings in symmetric representations

- **Source:** [OEIS A370206](https://oeis.org/A370206)
- **Date accessed:** 2026-10-15
- **Proposer:** Hartmut F. W. Hoft (conjecture; sequence added Feb 11 2024)
- **Area:** Divisor sums and combinatorial representations
- **Tractability score:** 1/5

## Statement

> The subsequence of numbers m whose two parts of SRS(m) meet at the diagonal is infinite.

Here SRS(m) means the symmetric representation of the sum of divisors of m, as used by the OEIS entry. The question can be restated as whether infinitely many m have the two halves of that representation meet at its diagonal.

## Partial results and resolution search

The OEIS comments give a necessary structural form for terms: 2^k*p*q with inequalities on the primes p,q. They also identify a family of smallest examples when q=2^(k+1)*p+1. These are partial structural observations, not an infinitude proof.

## Why it may be tractable

The OEIS reduces membership to explicit factorization and inequalities. A director could first ask whether that criterion yields a simple parametric family.

## Limits and cautions

This is the weakest fit in the shortlist. “SRS” needs a specialized definition, and infinitude may require proving that certain prime patterns recur. Do not choose this as an evening target unless the structural reduction reveals a direct construction.
