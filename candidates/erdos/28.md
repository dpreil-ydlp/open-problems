# Erdős Problem 28

- **URL:** https://www.erdosproblems.com/28
- **Prize:** $500
- **Status:** OPEN (informal status in the external database; accessed 2026-10-15)
- **Tags / area:** number theory, additive basis
- **Date accessed:** 2026-10-15

## Statement (verbatim)

```text
If $A\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is such that $A+A$ contains all but finitely many integers then $\limsup 1_A\ast 1_A(n)=\infty$.
```

## Site remarks, known results, and literature (verbatim)

```text
Conjectured by Erd\H{o}s and Tur\'{a}n. They also suggest the stronger conjecture that $\limsup  1_A\ast 1_A(n)/\log n>0$.

Another stronger conjecture would be that the hypothesis $\lvert A\cap [1,N]\rvert \gg N^{1/2}$ for all large $N$ suffices.

See also [40], and [1145] for a stronger generalisation.

This is discussed in problem C9 of Guy's collection \cite{Gu04}.
```

## Luna notes

- **Open direction [inferred]:** prove an unbounded lower bound for the pair-sum representation function [inferred].
- **Best known partial result [copied summary]:** Conjectured by Erd\H{o}s and Tur\'{a}n. They also suggest the stronger conjecture that $\limsup  1_A\ast 1_A(n)/\log n>0$.

See also [40], and [1145] for a stronger generalisation.

This is discussed in problem C9 of Guy's collection \cite{Gu04}.

## Recent literature

“Erdős Problem 28”; “Erdős–Turán additive bases conjecture”; “representation function unbounded additive basis”; queried arXiv, Google Scholar (2023–2026), and MathOverflow.

- **arXiv (2026-05-29): [An Improvement of Konstantoulas’ Density Constant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30922).** Improves density conditions for the Erdős–Turán additive-bases problem; proves a finite lower bound on the limsup representation count under specified missing-set density hypotheses. It does not prove unboundedness.
- **Google Scholar (2026-08-27): [On the Erdős-Turán Additive Bases Conjecture](https://maths-proofs.edounze.com/pdfs/erdos_turan.pdf).** The paper gives the classical random construction with representation counts of logarithmic order and explicitly says the conjecture remains unsolved; it does not prove the requested unboundedness.
