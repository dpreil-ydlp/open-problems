## Statement (verbatim)

```text
Let $A\subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be a set of $n$ points. Must there be two distances which occur at least once but between at most $n$ pairs of points? Must the number of such distances $\to \infty$ as $n\to \infty$?
```

## Site remarks, known results, and literature (verbatim)

```text
Asked by Erd\H{o}s and Pach. Hopf and Pannowitz \cite{HoPa34} proved that the largest distance between points of $A$ can occur at most $n$ times, but it is unknown whether a second such distance must occur.

It may be true that there are at least $n^{1-o(1)}$ many such distances. In \cite{Er97e} Erd\H{o}s offers \$100 for 'any nontrivial result'.

Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er84c} believed that for $n\geq 5$ there must always exist at least two such distances. This is false for $n=4$, as witnessed by two equilateral triangles of the same side-length glued together. Erd\H{o}s and Fishburn \cite{ErFi95} proved this is true for $n=5$ and $n=6$.

Clemen, Dumitrescu, and Liu \cite{CDL25} have proved that there always at least two such distances if $A$ is in convex position (that is, no point lies inside the convex hull of the others). They also prove it is true if the set $A$ is 'not too convex', in a specific technical sense.

See also [223], [756], and [957].
```

## Comments (verbatim)

### Comment 1

> An improvement on the lower bound in Problem 1.6 of Clemen–Dumitrescu–Liu (arXiv:2505.04283), the extremal question behind this problem: they showed min{μ(Δ₂), μ(δ)} can reach (9/8)n and asked for the optimal constant (upper bound 3/2 by Vesztergombi). We
> raise the lower bound to (9/7)n − O(√n): for an even regular m-gon, m = 2⌈3n/14⌉, the standard "server" points u_i ∈ C(v_i, Δ₂) ∩ C(v_{i+1}, Δ₂) sit under edge midpoints of the antipodal arc and each collects TWO exact minimum-distance pairs (for odd m — the
> previously used case — they sit under a vertex and collect only one chain pair), changing the trade-off from min(3m, 3n−5m) to min(3m, 3n−4m); a triangular-lattice patch at mesh equal to the flank distance completes the construction. We conjecture 9/7 is
> optimal. Write-up with complete proofs and a single-command verification script: https://github.com/mathematiclover1337/erdos-132-note (run python3 code/verify_all.py).
> 
> The note also contains an independent, differently-structured proof of the cases n = 7, 8, 9, 10, 13 of the conjecture (n = 8, 10 via circle-intersection enumeration against R₇, R₉) — we found these before seeing the earlier comments here, and priority for the
> small cases belongs to Marche's July note (and to Zeraoulia Rafik for n = 7); we keep ours only as independent confirmation. One further remark: the January comment's GPT-5.2 argument for n = 8 relies on a classification of 8-point 4-distance sets that does
> not exist in the literature (sub-maximum k-distance sets are unclassified); both Marche's proof and ours avoid this gap, so the n = 8 case does hold, but via the descent arguments rather than that route.
