## Statement (verbatim)

```text
What is $\mathrm{ex}_3(n,K_4^3)$? That is, the largest number of $3$-edges which can placed on $n$ vertices so that there exists no $K_4^3$, a set of 4 vertices which is covered by all 4 possible $3$-edges.
```

## Site remarks, known results, and literature (verbatim)

```text
A problem of Tur\'{a}n. Tur\'{a}n observed that dividing the vertices into three equal parts $X_1,X_2,X_3$, and taking the edges to be those triples that either have exactly one vertex in each part or two vertices in $X_i$ and one vertex in $X_{i+1}$ (where $X_4=X_1$) shows that\[\mathrm{ex}_3(n,K_4^3)\geq\left(\frac{5}{9}+o(1)\right)\binom{n}{3}.\]This is probably the truth. The current best upper bound is\[\mathrm{ex}_3(n,K_4^3)\leq 0.5611666\binom{n}{3},\]due to Razborov \cite{Ra10}.

See also [712] for the general case.
```

## Comments (verbatim)

### Comment 1

> An improved upper bound: $\pi(K_4^3) \le 0.561465$
> 
> First, the record: the best previously available upper bound for the Turán density $\pi(K_4^3)$ is not the 0.561666 this page currently lists. Razborov's 0.561666 (SIAM J. Discrete Math. 24 (2010) 946–963) was improved in 2012 by Rahil Baber to 0.5615, in §5.1 of "Turán densities of hypercubes" (arXiv:1201.3587v2) — a side result in a paper about hypercubes, never journal-published, and the $K_4^3$ bound appears only in v2; recent work still cites 0.561666 (e.g. arXiv:2511.02864). Baber's proof is a flag-algebra certificate (K4.txt in the arXiv source tarball): 83 positive-semidefinite blocks over a coloured 6-vertex universe, 4,822 regularity multipliers, and 123 counting multipliers, with entries printed as exact rationals with denominators of order $10^{27}$.
> 
> Two observations, in increasing order of work.
> 
> First, a sharper reading of his certificate as printed. The target density $\rho$ enters it in exactly one place, affinely: the regularity rows carry a factor $([\text{root } \theta \text{ is blue}] - \rho)$, so each of the 49,242 constraint coefficients has the form $a_H - \rho \cdot b_H$ with $b_H > 0$, and each constraint object $H$ yields a threshold $\rho_H = a_H/b_H$. The certificate proves $\pi(K_4^3) < \rho$ for every $\rho$ exceeding $\rho^* = \max_H \rho_H = 0.56146562\ldots$, hence $\pi(K_4^3) \le \rho^*$. His stated 0.5615 is the smallest four-decimal value above $\rho^*$: the binary search stopped at four decimals, and the certificate always proved more. (By "his 0.5615" we mean the value stated in arXiv:1201.3587v2 §5.1.)
> 
> Second, better multipliers exist in his own certificate family — the same 44 types, the same 4,822 regularity flags, the same 123 counting graphs, the same semantics; only the numbers are new. Searching the same feasible certificate family, while retaining his point as an incumbent, yields multipliers whose exact threshold is $0.56146468722\ldots$ (with $\pi(K_4^3) < \rho$ for every $\rho$ above it), and we claim the round figure
> 
> $\pi(K_4^3) \le 112293/200000 = 0.561465,$
> 
> an improvement of $3.5 \times 10^{-5}$ over the published 0.5615 and strictly below the ceiling $\rho^*$ of the printed certificate. For scale: the gap from 0.5615 down to Turán's conjectured $5/9 \approx 0.5556$ is $5.9 \times 10^{-3}$, so this closes about 0.6% of what remains — a better certificate inside an existing method, not new mathematics about the conjecture.
> 
> What was verified, and how. Nothing here rests on trusting our reading of the certificate file. Exact rational arithmetic end to end: every one of the 49,242 coefficients is evaluated as an exact fraction at exact rational $\rho$, and at $\rho = 0.561465$ every coefficient is strictly negative. The new certificate's blocks are positive semidefinite by construction (each is an integer Gram matrix divided by an integer square) and additionally proved so by exact elimination with a different pivoting order; Baber's own 83 blocks were proved PSD the same two ways. Two independent implementations of every term (different triple indexings, different canonicalisations; the PSD row additionally reimplemented directly from the statement of the averaging lemma, sharing no canonicalisation, flag table, split enumeration or normaliser with the main pipeline; exact agreement on both binding objects). The averaging argument itself is proved, not cited: Baber writes the chain in full only for hypercubes, and we prove the 3-graph instantiation directly — the counting rows are an exact identity at every finite $n$ (a two-line double count via the pair-exchange involution, normaliser $1/15 = 1/\binom{6}{2}$), the regularity rows are $o(1)$ under almost-regularity (normaliser $1/6$ = the uniform law of the root), the PSD rows average to a nonnegative quantity, and a strictly negative maximum contradicts $\rho \le \pi(K_4^3)$. No coloured-to-ordinary transfer step exists in this instantiation: the colouring is a bookkeeping record of six of the ambient graph's own triples. Controls: at Baber's own $\rho = 0.5615$ the same evaluation confirms his certificate (maximum coefficient $\approx -3437.18$, all 49,242 strictly negative); inverting the file's colour convention yields $\rho^* \approx 1.5615$ — true but vacuous — and the convention is fixed four independent ways, including a purely structural one (the 123 printed counting graphs form a clean transversal of the pair-exchange involution only under the correct reading). The certificate file's own problem statement (forbidden family = $K_4^3$, ordinary containment, order 6) is parsed and asserted, with negative controls on mutated files.
> 
> What is not claimed. No progress on Turán's conjecture proper: the bound moves the fifth decimal, the conjectured value is $5/9$, and the method is exactly Baber's — we contribute the observation that his certificate proved more than he claimed, a modest further optimisation of its multipliers, and the verification apparatus.
> 
> The complete verification suite (exact evaluators, both independent implementations, the semantics document with proofs, and the checker for Baber's file) is at github.com/bitterlemma/erdos-500 (MIT; the README gives the one command that re-derives $\rho^*$ from K4.txt and the one command that verifies the new certificate).

### Comment 2

> Two notes on the displayed upper bound, both checked against the primary sources.
> 
> First, the number itself: the page states "the current best upper bound is $\mathrm{ex}3(n, K4^{(3)}) \le 0.5611666 \binom{n}{3}$, due to Razborov [Ra10]". That value has an extra digit and matches no number in the literature: Razborov's paper (SIAM J. Discrete Math. 24 (2010) 946–963, pp. 947–948) gives $\pi{\min}(I4^3) \ge 0.438334$, whose complement is $0.561666$. Razborov also presents it as a numerical suggestion rather than a theorem — his wording is "our numerical computations suggest the following improvement".
> 
> Second, and more substantially: the record has not been $0.561666$ since 2012. Baber, "Turán densities of hypercubes", arXiv:1201.3587v2 (13 Nov 2012), Section 5.1: "The best known bound was held by Razborov [18] at 0.56167 by considering 3-graphs of order 6. We can decrease this to 0.5615 by looking at red-blue vertex-coloured 3-graphs of order 6, together with regularity constraints as described by Hladký, Král', and Norine." The same section points at the certificate: "The relevant data required to prove the 0.5615 bound can be found in K4.txt located in the source files section on the arXiv." (Cite arXiv:1201.3587, Baber sole author — not the Baber–Talbot paper arXiv:1110.4287, which does not contain this bound. 1201.3587 carries no journal reference on arXiv, which may be why it is under-cited.)
> 
> As far as we can find, $0.5615$ is still the record: the survey "Hypergraph Turán Problems in $\ell2$-Norm" (arXiv:2108.10406) lists $5/9 \le \pi(K4^{(3)}) \le 0.5615$, and no later improvement surfaced in our search. The stale $0.561666$ appears to be genuinely under-known — it is quoted as the current bound as recently as arXiv:2511.02864, Section 6.20 (Nov 2025). So this is less a typo report than a pointer at a fourteen-year-old improvement the field seems to have missed.
