## Statement (verbatim)

```text
Let $h(N)$ be the maximum size of a Sidon set in $\{1,\ldots,N\}$. Is it true that, for every $\epsilon>0$,\[h(N) = N^{1/2}+O_\epsilon(N^\epsilon)?\]
```

## Site remarks, known results, and literature (verbatim)

```text
A problem of Erd\H{o}s and Tur\'{a}n. It may even be true that $h(N)=N^{1/2}+O(1)$, but Erd\H{o}s remarks this is perhaps too optimistic. Erd\H{o}s and Tur\'{a}n \cite{ErTu41} proved an upper bound of $N^{1/2}+O(N^{1/4})$, with an alternative proof by Lindstr\"{o}m \cite{Li69}. Both proofs in fact give\[h(N) \leq N^{1/2}+N^{1/4}+1.\]Balogh, F\"{u}redi, and Roy \cite{BFR21} improved the bound in the error term to $0.998N^{1/4}$. This was further optimised by O'Bryant \cite{OB22}. The current record is\[h(N)\leq N^{1/2}+0.98183N^{1/4}+O(1),\]due to Carter, Hunter, and O'Bryant \cite{CHO25}.

Singer \cite{Si38} was the first to show that $h(N)\geq (1-o(1))N^{1/2}$ for all $N$. For a detailed survey of the literature we refer to \cite{OB04}.

See also [241] and [840].

This problem is Problem 31 on Green's open problems list.

This is discussed in problem C9 of Guy's collection \cite{Gu04}.
```

### Comment 1

> Update on the second-order constant: a verified certificate giving
> $$h(N) \le N^{1/2} + 0.9428096\,N^{1/4} + O(1).$$
> 
> This is below the 0.9435 of Hou–Zhao (arXiv:2607.01169, later version) and well below the 0.98183 currently listed on the problem page. It comes from the Hou–Zhao vector-valued smoothing framework, extended to allow asymmetric kernels. The certificate uses the linear kernel $\rho(x)=2x$ at resolution m = 512 with boundary depth L = 8. All 8,194 covering inequalities and the constants $a$, $b$ and $\gamma^2=ab$ were checked in exact rational arithmetic, giving $\gamma < 0.942809522$.
> 
> The same work shows this is essentially the end of the road for the method: every certificate in the framework, symmetric or two-sided, has $\gamma \ge 2\sqrt{2}/3 = 0.9428090\ldots$, and two-sided certificates attain it. So further optimisation inside the framework can gain at most about $5\times10^{-7}$, and the method cannot improve the exponent 1/4. Going below $2\sqrt{2}/3$ would have to keep the variance term in Cilleruelo's identity or the slack in the small-difference bound. A pair-level semidefinite relaxation that keeps both has the same constant.
> 
> The bound rests on Hou–Zhao Theorem 2.1 and on our two-sided extension of it, which is proved in full in the companion paper (Appendix C). None of this has been refereed or posted to arXiv yet. It was developed with substantial help from Anthropic's Claude models, which is credited in both documents. I'd welcome independent checking of the certificate and the proof of the lower bound; the certificates and verifier are available on request.
> 
> Report (computational and analytic, including the certificate): https://github.com/johnakwei/Science/blob/main/A_Data_Science_Analysis_of_Erdos_Problem_30.pdf
> Companion paper (proof of the $2\sqrt{2}/3$ limit): https://github.com/johnakwei/Science/blob/main/The_limit_of_vector-valued_smoothing_for_Sidon_sets.pdf

### Comment 2

> **An improved constant in the second-order term: h(N) ≤ N^(1/2) + 0.94325·N^(1/4) + O(1)**
> 
> Two updates for this thread, neither of which it currently mentions:
> 
> 1. Hou and Zhao (arXiv:2607.01169, July 2026) proved h(N) ≤ N^(1/2) + 0.9435·N^(1/4) + O(1), via a finite-certificate framework (their Lemma 2.1) with a published exact-arithmetic verifier. This improves the 0.97633 value discussed above (and the tentative ~0.947 mentioned in February).
> 
> 2. Working inside their framework, I have an improved certificate:
> 
> γ = 0.943244449828… < 0.94325,  i.e.  h(N) ≤ N^(1/2) + 0.94325·N^(1/4) + O(1),
> 
> a 2.5×10⁻⁴ improvement over Hou–Zhao. A fallback certificate at the paper's own m=32, L=4 grid setting gives γ = 0.943260253…, in case the primary certificate's slightly extended setting (L=5, histogram kernels — which I read as within Lemma 2.1's stated scope) concerns anyone.
> 
> Verification: two independent exact-rational checkers (Python 3.9+, stdlib only, under 5 seconds), a mutation-test harness of negative controls with SHA-pinned runner, and a Lean 4 + Mathlib kernel-checked verification of all 161 covering inequalities (zero sorries, standard axioms only). The honest caveat, stated plainly: like the Hou–Zhao bound itself, this result is exactly as strong as their Lemma 2.1, which is weeks old and not yet peer-reviewed. That lemma is the load-bearing item needing expert eyes.
> 
> Method, briefly: Hou–Zhao's own search is greedy and stage-wise; rerun at 7.5× their evaluation budget it fails to re-find their own optimum. Jointly optimizing the outer kernel and mixing parameters (with boundary vectors re-solved by an inner quadratic program) passes it immediately. The search, rationalization, dual verification, and adversarial review were carried out by an autonomous multi-agent Claude campaign I directed.
> 
> A 6-page note with both certificates and both verifiers attached as ancillary files is prepared for submission to arXiv. The certificate package is also available by email: [email protected]. I contacted Prof. Tao and Prof. O'Bryant with the package on 12 August, and I am contacting Hou and Zhao.

### Comment 3

> A small data note on the exact values of h(N). Since a Sidon subset of {1,...,N} of size k is equivalent to a Golomb ruler with k marks and length at most N-1, and optimal Golomb ruler lengths are known exactly through 28 marks (OGR-28, length 585), h(N) is exactly determined for all N <= 586. I have extended the b-file of OEIS A143824 (which previously stopped at N=500) to N=586 on this basis, after reproducing all 501 published terms from the correspondence and re-verifying the optimal ruler lengths for k <= 13 with an independent search program.
> 
> Against the current record upper bound h(N) <= N^{1/2} + 0.98183 N^{1/4} + O(1) of Carter-Hunter-O'Bryant: on this (admittedly tiny) range the observed gap h(N) - sqrt(N) grows from 1.93 at N=50 to 3.79 at N=586, i.e. it tracks N^{1/4} closely (the ratio to 0.98183 N^{1/4} oscillates between about 0.50 and 0.94, with a local peak of 0.937 at N=56). In particular the data at this scale shows the gap still increasing, so the optimistic h(N) = N^{1/2} + O(1) is not yet visible in exact values, though of course N <= 586 says nothing about the N^epsilon conjecture itself. Exact values, the derivation, and the verification code are in the OEIS entry and I am happy to share details.
