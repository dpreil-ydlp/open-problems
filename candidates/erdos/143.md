# Erdős Problem 143

- **URL:** https://www.erdosproblems.com/143
- **Prize:** $500
- **Status:** OPEN (informal status in the external database; accessed 2026-10-15)
- **Tags / area:** primitive sets
- **Date accessed:** 2026-10-15

## Statement (verbatim)

```text
Let $A\subset (1,\infty)$ be a countably infinite set such that for all $x\neq y\in A$ and integers $k\geq 1$ we have\[ \lvert kx -y\rvert \geq 1.\]Does this imply that $A$ is sparse? In particular, does this imply that\[\sum_{x\in A}\frac{1}{x\log x}<\infty\]or\[\sum_{\substack{x <n\\ x\in A}}\frac{1}{x}=o(\log n)?\]
```

## Site remarks, known results, and literature (verbatim)

```text
Note that if $A$ is a set of integers then the condition implies that $A$ is a primitive set (that is, no element of $A$ is divisible by any other), for which the convergence of $\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{n\log n}$ was proved by Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er35}, and the upper bound\[\sum_{\substack{x <n\\ x\in A}}\frac{1}{n}\ll \frac{\log x}{\sqrt{\log\log x}}\]was proved by Behrend \cite{Be35}. This $O(\cdot)$ bound was improved to a $o(\cdot)$ bound by Erd\H{o}s, S\'{a}rk\H{o}zy, and Szemer\'{e}di \cite{ESS67}.

In \cite{Er73} and \cite{Er77c} Erd\H{o}s mentions an unpublished proof of Haight that\[\lim \frac{\lvert A\cap [1,x]\rvert}{x}=0\]holds if the elements of $A$ are independent over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Over the years Erd\H{o}s asked for various different quantitative estimates, for example\[\liminf \frac{\lvert A\cap [1,x]\rvert}{x}=0\]or even (motivated by Behrend's bound)\[\sum_{\substack{x <n\\ x\in A}}\frac{1}{x}\ll \frac{\log x}{\sqrt{\log\log x}}.\]In \cite{Er97c} he offers \$500 for resolving the questions in the main problem statement above.

This was partially resolved by Koukoulopoulos, Lamzouri, and Lichtman \cite{KLL25}, who proved that we must have\[\sum_{\substack{x <n\\ x\in A}}\frac{1}{x}=o(\log n).\]See also [858].
```

### Comment 2

> I think I can use the flow method used in one of the proofs of [1196] to reprove Behrend's bound $\sum_{n \in A; n \leq x} \frac{1}{n} \ll \frac{\log x}{\sqrt{\log\log x}}$ when $A$ consists of natural numbers (incidentally, the constraint $n \in A$ is currently missing in the statement of Behrend's bound).
> 
> On the squarefree numbers $n \leq x$, create a flow by setting $w(np \mapsto n) = \frac{1}{np} \frac{\omega(n)!}{(\sum_{p \leq x} 1/p)^{\omega(n)+1}}$, where $\omega(n)$ is the number of prime factors of $n$.  At any $1 < n \leq x$, the net outflow of $n$ is $\frac{1}{n} \frac{\omega(n)!}{(\sum_{p \leq x} 1/p)^{\omega(n)}}$, which is at least the net inflow; and at $1$ the net inflow is at most $1$.  The discrete divergence theorem then gives the inequality
> $$ \sum_{n \in A, n \leq x, \mathrm{squarefree}} \frac{1}{n} \frac{\omega(n)!}{(\sum_{p \leq x} 1/p)^{\omega(n)}} \leq 1. \tag{1}$$
> From Stirling's formula and Mertens' theorem one has $\frac{\omega(n)!}{(\sum_{p \leq x} 1/p)^{\omega(n)}} \gg \frac{\sqrt{\log\log x}}{\log x}$, giving Behrend's inequality for the squarefree $n$.  (This gain over Behrend is analogous to the gain of the LYM inequality over Sperner's inequality.) The remaining $n$ can be dealt with by factoring out the maximal square factor $d^2$ and achieving a similar bound (with a gain of $1/d^2$) for each such value of $d$.
> 
> The Stirling formula also produces a factor of $\sqrt{2\pi}$ that also appears in a refinement of Behrend's bound proven by Erdos, Sarkozy, and Szemeredi.  This is probably not a coincidence.
> 
> I think the same sort of argument works to give the more general estimate of Ahlswede, Khatchatrian, and Sarkozy that $\sum_{n \in A, y \leq n \leq yx} \frac{1}{n} \ll \frac{\log x}{\sqrt{\log\log x}}$ for any $x,y \geq 1$ and primitive set $A$ of integers; one now has to restrict $\omega(n)$ to only count prime factors up to $x$.
> 
> EDIT: I can remove the squarefree restriction in (1), thus giving the Erdos-Sarkozy-Szemeredi bound $\sum_{n \in A; n \leq x} \frac{1}{n} \leq (\sqrt{2\pi}+o(1)) \frac{\log x}{\sqrt{\log\log x}}$ from Stirling and Mertens.  Here it is convenient to go back to the Markov chain approach.  Consider a chain that starts at $1$ and jumps from $n$ to $nq$ with probability $\frac{1/q}{\sum_{q' \leq x} 1/q'}$ where $q,q'$ are restricted to be prime powers that are at most $x$.  This chain hits $A$ at most once, but each $n$ is hit with probability at least $\frac{1}{n} \frac{\omega(n)!}{(\sum_{q \leq x} 1/q)^{\omega(n)}}$, by considering the $\omega(n)!$ ways to split $n$ as the product of distinct prime powers.  The claim follows.  UPDATE: Actually it looks like one doesn't quite get the sharp constant $\sqrt{2\pi}$ by this approach.
